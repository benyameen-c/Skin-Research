## Damage scan: what is damaged in IGH white spots

<p><span class="tag lead">Leads only</span> Not in the evidence ledger. Not medical advice. Gate A has not been passed for any source here.</p>

### How to read this page

Every claim below carries a verbatim quote and a PMID. The quotes come from abstracts or open-access full texts saved in the project's excerpts folder (`pmid_<id>.txt`, `pmc_<id>.txt`). Most sources here were read as abstracts only, so their numbers, methods and caveats are `not in abstract` until a full text is read. Sentences marked **Inference** are this page's reasoning, not a finding from any paper.

Most IGH studies are small (often 3 to 20 people) and descriptive. None tested a cause by changing it and watching the spot.

### What the spots look like under the microscope

The most repeated finding is less melanin and fewer pigment cells (melanocytes) in the spot than in the skin around it, with a thin, flattened top layer.

- Joshi 2014, 55 biopsies (PMID 25484386): "The epidermis had markedly decreased to absent melanin in the basal layer and reduced numbers of melanocytes at the dermoepidermal junction." It also reports "small foci of retained melanin in the basal layer (skip areas) alternating with larger areas of melanin loss were present in almost 80% of cases."
- Kim 2010, 47 patients, lesion compared with skin next to it (PMID 20465639): "The IGH lesions demonstrated decreased melanin pigment and reduced numbers of melanocytes by NKI/beteb and MART-1." The flattened skin was less common than older reports said: "epidermal atrophy was present in only five cases (10.6%)".
- Falabella 1987 (PMID 3805391): "Epithelial atrophy, patchy absence of melanocytes and melanin, flattening of the rete pegs, and basket weave hyperkeratosis were the most prominent histologic findings".
- Reddy 2024, 100 patients (PMID 39324349): "reduced melanin globules or melanocytes, skip lesions, and flattening of rete ridges across all dermoscopic patterns."

### What happens to the pigment cells

The sources agree that melanocytes are fewer. They disagree on whether the ones left are healthy.

- Fewer cells, not stuck immature cells. Wallace 1998, nine lesions (PMID 9765023): "the pathogenesis of IGH involves an absolute decrease in the number of melanocytes; a block in melanocyte differentiation does not appear to be a major component of the process."
- Two stages. Ortonne 1980, three patients (PMID 7377803): "Depigmentation in IGH seems to occur in two stages, loss of melanogenic activity in certain malanocytes and elimination of inactive melanocytes." (The misspelling is in the abstract.)
- Not all gone. Wilson 1982, 20 cases (PMID 6183861): "In no case were melanocytes completely absent, excluding vitiligo."
- Fewer and smaller pigment granules. Savall 1980 (PMID 6779850): "a decreased number of melanosomes in the melanocytes with predominance of Stages I and II and small sizes." Kim 2010 (PMID 20465639): "The ultrastructural evaluation showed degenerative melanocytes and decreased melanosomes."
- Structurally normal cells. Kakepis 2015, four patients (PMID 25088925): "No significant structural abnormality of the melanocytes was observed".
- Counts. Gilhar 1989 (PMID 2508572) gives dopa-positive melanocytes in the spot versus the surrounding skin: "55.25 +/- 18.00 mm2 vs 220 +/- 28.28 mm2". Ploysangam 1990 (PMID 2229495): "significantly fewer dopa-positive melanocytes are in the white macules of idiopathic guttate hypomelanosis than in normal skin."
- A newer letter titled "Melanocytes in idiopathic guttate hypomelanosis disappear and are senescent" (Kim 2023, PMID 36394380) has no abstract; its content is `full text needed` (on the paywall list).

### Pigment transfer to the surrounding skin cells

Melanocytes make pigment and hand it to the surrounding skin cells (keratinocytes), which carry the colour. Three sources point to a fault in that hand-over, as well as in cell numbers.

- Kakepis 2015 (PMID 25088925): "In some areas, there was a reduced uptake of melanosomes by the keratinocytes." Its conclusion: "a functional defect in the transfer of melanosomes from the melanocytes to the keratinocytes."
- Rani 2018, six patients and six controls (PMID 29516488): "Melanocytes from IGH patients were bigger in size with very small and retracted dendrites as compared to the control melanocytes." The authors suggest pigment builds up inside the cell: "Accumulation of more melanin in the IGH melanocytes might be due to problem in the transfer of melanin from melanocytes to keratinocytes." That sentence is the authors' hypothesis, and these cells were grown in a dish.
- Arbache 2022, 20 patients, lesion compared with skin next to it (PMID 35114009): "unreported keratinocyte cytoplasmic changes on electron microscopy." Details are `full text needed`.
- Senescent melanocytes in a dish show the same pattern. Park 2023, open access, full text read (PMID 37554281): "in vitro senescent melanocytes are characterized by melanosome transport dysfunction resulting in melanin accumulation". The in-body check rests on one person: "We confirmed these findings in vivo in skin biopsies from a donor with IGH."

### Senescence, oxidative stress and the dermis

Senescent cells are cells that have permanently stopped dividing but stay in the tissue and release signals. Four IGH sources point this way, all small.

- Rani 2018 (PMID 29516488): "expression of p16, p21, and hp1 was significantly higher in lesional skin of IGH patient as compared to healthy controls." The comparison is with other people, not with the same person's normal skin.
- Park 2023 (PMID 37554281) cites an earlier finding, a secondary claim until the cited paper is read: "The number of p16Ink4a-expressing melanocytes was increased in hypopigmented IGH skin compared to perilesional normal skin".
- Kim 2026 (PMID 41453137), working in UV-damaged melanocytes in a dish: "ATG7 downregulation emerged as the earliest molecular alteration and was consistently found in senescent melanocytes and idiopathic guttate hypomelanosis skin." ATG7 is a gene needed for the cell's recycling system (autophagy). The same abstract reports that "Metformin treatment restored autophagic activity" in cultured cells; no IGH patient was treated. Human sample counts are `not in abstract`.
- Senescent fibroblasts (the dermis cells under the spot): Kim 2020, a letter titled "Role of senescent fibroblasts in the development of idiopathic guttate hypomelanosis" (PMID 31823346). No abstract; content is `full text needed`.
- Dermal scarring under the spot. Arbache 2022 (PMID 35114009): "We found superficial dermal fibrosis in 90% (18/20) of our IGH cases". The authors conclude: "Our results suggest an interaction between melanocytes and the subjacent dermis in IGH."
- Outside IGH, aged fibroblasts change pigment in lab-grown skin. Duval 2014 (PMID 25490395): "dermal fibroblasts do indeed impact the degree of skin pigmentation." That model showed more pigment, not less, with photo-aged fibroblasts: "A stimulation of pigmentation in the presence of the natural photo-aged fibroblasts was revealed".
- Oxidative stress. Zeeshan 2022, 30 patients, measured in blood (PMID 33894101): "the oxidative stress is increased in patients with IGH." Blood markers say nothing direct about the spot itself.
