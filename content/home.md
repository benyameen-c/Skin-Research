# Which treatments help a small white spot match the surrounding skin, and does the match last?

<p class="lede">Several treatments bring some colour back within four months. No study found so far has checked whether a treated spot still matched the surrounding skin six months after its last treatment.</p>

{{evidence_counts}}

## What the first session found

- **Durability is unmeasured.** In the evidence ledger, the longest quoted gap between the last treatment and an assessment on an IGH row is 2 months, after a single laser session, in two studies. shin2013: "Two months after a single treatment, physicians' clinical assessments were performed". goldust2013: "Two months after a single treatment session, 115 patients (47.9%) exhibited more than 75% clinical improvement." No ledger row measures a lasting colour match.
- **Too much pigment is a real outcome.** One excimer series left 18 of 200 spots darker than their surroundings. A peel arm produced scars in 3 of 75 spots.
- **Untreated and sham-treated spots also change.** Saline needling cut the median count of white spots from 31 to 21. Graders called 18% of untreated spots improved in a laser trial.
- **Nothing new in PubMed.** A dated PubMed search returned 20 records since the 2025 review's cutoff and none is a new IGH treatment trial. One comparison study outside PubMed was found.
- **Misalignment can fake fading.** In a simulation, placing the outline 0.25 mm off a 2 mm spot reads as 10 to 16% fading when nothing changed.

## How to read this site

Every statement carries one of these labels.

<p><span class="tag human">Published human finding</span> A result reported in a paper or trial registry, with a link to where the number appears.</p>
<p><span class="tag hypo">Hypothesis</span> A proposed explanation that fits some observations and has not been tested as a treatment.</p>
<p><span class="tag sim">Simulation</span> A calculation on synthetic shapes. It tests a measuring method and says nothing about patients.</p>
<p><span class="tag calc">Calculation on published counts</span> Arithmetic on numbers taken from papers, with the assumptions stated.</p>
